Essential Apps and Payments for Traveling in China

Last reviewed: 2026-10-27

Quick Answer: Before traveling to China, prepare: a payment app (Alipay or WeChat Pay), maps/navigation, a translation tool, a ride-hailing app, and a connectivity plan (eSIM or roaming). Read on for specific recommendations and setup steps.

Which Apps Do Foreign Visitors Need?

China's app ecosystem is different from most countries. Many Western apps don't work or aren't widely used. Here are the categories you should prepare for, based on what travelers actually need on the ground.

💳 Payments

China is largely cashless. You'll need at least one of these to pay for almost everything — meals, transport, shops, even street vendors.

  • Alipay (支付宝) Supports international Visa/Mastercard binding. Widely accepted everywhere in China. Official site ↗
  • WeChat Pay (微信支付) Built into WeChat. Also supports foreign cards for many users. Official site ↗

🗺️ Maps & Navigation

Google Maps has limited data in China. Plan your navigation strategy before arrival.

  • Apple Maps Works in mainland China with English place names. Best option for iPhone users.
  • Amap (高德) / Baidu Maps Most accurate local data, but Chinese-language only. Useful if you can read some Chinese.
  • Offline maps Download areas before departure as backup. Apps like Maps.me or organic maps work offline.

🚕 Ride-Hailing

  • DiDi (滴滴) Available in English via standalone app or WeChat mini-program. Works in most major Chinese cities.

🗣️ Translation

  • Phone built-in translator iOS and Android both include conversation translation. Good for menus and signs.
  • Papago Offline-capable translation app by Naver. Supports Chinese well and works without data.

💬 Communication

  • WeChat (微信) Essential in China — used for messaging, payments, business contact, and even venue check-in. Install before departure.

Note: WhatsApp, Telegram, and most Western messaging apps are not reliably accessible in mainland China.

Alipay & WeChat Pay Setup

Both Alipay and WeChat Pay now allow binding international credit and debit cards (Visa, Mastercard, JCB) for many users. However, acceptance varies by card issuer and bank — we cannot guarantee your specific card will work.

General Setup Steps

  1. Download the app (Alipay or WeChat) before departure
  2. Register with your phone number (international numbers are supported)
  3. Complete identity verification (passport photo may be required)
  4. Attempt to add your international card under "Bank Cards" or "Wallet"
  5. Test with a small transaction if possible
Not all foreign cards work. Some banks block transactions with Chinese payment platforms. If your card is rejected, try a different card network, contact your bank to enable international transactions, or carry some cash (RMB) as backup for your first few days.

Common Issues

  • Card rejected: Try a different card (Visa tends to work more widely than some regional debit cards). Contact your bank to confirm China/Alipay/WeChat transactions are permitted.
  • Transaction limits: Foreign-card transactions may have lower per-transaction or monthly limits. Check within the app for current limits.
  • 3D Secure verification: Some payments require SMS verification from your home bank — make sure your phone can receive SMS while abroad.
  • Name mismatch: Ensure the name on your payment account matches your passport exactly.

Connectivity & Internet

Having reliable internet access is critical for maps, payments, and translation. Here are the main options for staying connected in China:

📱 eSIM (Recommended for most travelers)

International eSIMs connect via roaming partners (China Unicom or China Mobile). They typically provide access to international websites and apps.

  • Airalo Popular, reliable, China Unicom network. Plans from ~$4.50.
  • Nomad Competitive pricing, flexible durations.
  • Holafly Unlimited data option for heavy users.

See our detailed guide: China eSIM Guide →

📶 International Roaming

Check with your home carrier about China roaming plans. Some carriers offer daily or trip-based passes. This is often more expensive than eSIM but requires no setup.

🏪 Local SIM Card

Available at airports and carrier stores with passport registration. Cheapest option for longer stays, but Chinese SIMs typically cannot access international websites.

About internet access in China: Mainland China has network restrictions that block many international websites and services. International eSIMs and roaming connections route through overseas partners, which generally provides access to services like Google, WhatsApp, and Instagram. We do not recommend or provide solutions that circumvent local regulations. Consult official sources for current information.

Before-You-Fly App Checklist

Go through this list before your trip. Don't wait until you land — airport WiFi can be unreliable, and you'll want everything ready when you arrive.

  • Install WeChat and create account
  • Install Alipay and try binding your international card
  • Test WeChat Pay with your card (if using WeChat for payments)
  • Install DiDi (or access via WeChat mini-program)
  • Download a translation app (Papago or phone built-in)
  • Download offline maps for your destination areas
  • Purchase and install eSIM (or arrange roaming with carrier)
  • Save hotel address in Chinese characters (screenshot or note)
  • Notify your bank about international travel
  • Bring a power adapter (China uses Type A/C/I, 220V)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Alipay or WeChat Pay with a foreign credit card?

Both Alipay and WeChat Pay support binding international Visa and Mastercard for many users, but acceptance varies by card issuer and bank. We recommend trying to bind your card before departure and having a backup payment method (cash or a second card).

Do I need WeChat to travel in China?

WeChat is widely used in China for messaging, payments, and sometimes venue check-in. It is highly recommended but not legally required. Many travelers manage with Alipay alone for payments, plus a translation app and maps.

What maps app works in China for foreigners?

Apple Maps works in mainland China and displays English place names. Amap (Gaode) and Baidu Maps have the most up-to-date local data but are Chinese-language only. Download offline maps before departure as backup.

Is DiDi available in English?

Yes. DiDi offers an English interface via its standalone app and through a WeChat mini-program. It works in most major Chinese cities including Shenzhen, Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou, and Chengdu.

What should I do if my card is rejected by Alipay or WeChat Pay?

Try a different card network (Visa, Mastercard, or JCB). Contact your bank to confirm international transactions are enabled. Check that your card supports 3D Secure verification. As a backup, carry some RMB cash for initial expenses until you get payments working.
